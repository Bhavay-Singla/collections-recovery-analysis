BOTTOM LINE
Recovery improved in July, but the data does not support a clean “11% operational improvement” claim.

Clean total recovery increased by about 6.7% from June to July. Some targeted-portfolio measures improved by 9–12%, but the same-account cohort improved by only 1.3%. This points to a mix/targeting effect, not an 11% across-the-board improvement.

What changed

The evidence points more toward targeting/mix than better calling operations.

New July targets performed betterJuly-only targeted accounts recovered more per account than June-only targets.
Contact rate fellCalls were not connecting better, even while recovery went up.
Same-account uplift was smallAccounts present in both months improved only around 1.3%.
Result depends on the metricDifferent valid definitions produce roughly 6.7% to 12.3% improvement.

Data issues leadership should know

These do not make the analysis useless, but they limit what we can claim confidently.

Duplicate payment eventsPayment IDs repeat and can overstate recovery if we simply sum raw rows.
Borrower IDs conflictEvent-level borrower IDs often disagree with the account master, so account ID is the safer key.
Call attribution is unreliableAttempt/disposition account IDs often do not match the linked call, so RPC/PTP-by-call claims are low confidence.
August is incompleteOnly eight days are available, so it should not be compared with a full month.
